# Hunza Valley — Viewpoints & Photography

Hunza is arguably the most photogenic valley in Pakistan, with Rakaposhi (7,788 m) dominating nearly every vantage point.

**Eagle's Nest** is the classic sunrise viewpoint. A 30-minute jeep ride above Karimabad brings you to a ridge at roughly 2,900 m where the first light hits Rakaposhi, Diran, and the Spantik-Sosbun massif. Arrive by 5:30 AM in summer; the road is accessible by 4x4 only and becomes treacherous after rain.

**Altit Fort viewpoint** offers the best composition of the fort itself against the Hunza River and snow peaks. The golden hour here is roughly 6:00–7:00 PM in July. Entry to the fort grounds is PKR 800 for foreigners, PKR 400 for locals; the viewpoint outside the walls is free.

**Attabad Lake's northern shore** (15 minutes east of Karimabad) provides mirror-reflection shots of the Passu Cathedral peaks on windless mornings. The best spot is near the tunnel entrance where a small gravel beach allows tripod setup. Go before 8 AM — the wind picks up by mid-morning.

**Passu Glacier viewpoint** on the KKH, roughly 45 minutes past Karimabad, frames the famous "Passu Cathedral" spires. Late afternoon side-lighting (4:00–6:00 PM) brings out the ridges. A wide-angle lens is essential — the peaks are deceptively close but span a massive field of view.

**Photography tips:** The valley haze builds by 10 AM; shoot early. Polarizing filters help cut atmospheric haze on peak shots. Drone usage requires NOC approval in Gilgit-Baltistan — do not fly without permits.

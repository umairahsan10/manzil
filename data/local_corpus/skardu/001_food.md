# Skardu — Local Food & Dining

Skardu town has evolved from a remote transit hub into a surprisingly diverse food destination, with options ranging from traditional Balti cuisine to backpacker-friendly cafes.

**Balti cuisine** is the highlight. The signature dish is **Balti mutton karahi** — slow-cooked in a wok-like vessel with local dried tomatoes, garlic, and mountain herbs. The **Baltistan Restaurant** on Sadpara Road is the most reliable spot, serving generous karahis for PKR 800–1,200. Their chapli kebab platter is also well-regarded.

**Shangrila Resort** (the "Lower Kachura Lake" resort) has the most polished dining experience in the region. The restaurant serves Pakistani and Chinese fusion; the lake-view terrace is worth the slightly higher prices (PKR 1,500–2,500 per meal). Non-guests can dine with a day-pass of PKR 500.

For budget travellers, **Cafe One** near the main bazaar serves decent burgers, pasta, and coffee — a rarity in GB. It is popular with trekkers preparing for K2 base camp. **The Khaplu Palace Serena** restaurant is open to day visitors and offers a refined take on traditional dishes in a historic setting (45 minutes from Skardu).

**Local specialities to try:**
- **Mamtu** — steamed meat dumplings, similar to manti, sold at street stalls
- **Balay** — noodle soup with yak meat (seasonal, winter months)
- **Apricot juice** — Skardu's orchards produce some of the sweetest apricots in GB

**Practical notes:** Alcohol is not available. Fresh produce is limited in early season (May); by July the local vegetable gardens are productive. Water purification is advisable — most restaurants use filtered water, but confirm if you have a sensitive stomach.

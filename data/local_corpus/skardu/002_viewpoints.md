# Skardu — Viewpoints & Photography

Skardu's landscape is defined by the dramatic confluence of the Indus and Shigar rivers, surrounded by 7,000+ metre peaks including K2 (visible on clear days from specific vantage points).

**Kharphocho Fort** (Skardu Fort) is the most accessible viewpoint. The 16th-century fort sits on a ridge above town and offers a 360-degree panorama of the Indus Valley, Skardu town, and the snow-capped peaks to the northeast. The climb from the road takes 20–30 minutes; go at sunrise (5:30 AM in July) for the clearest atmospheric conditions.

**Upper Kachura Lake** is the less-visited sister of the famous Shangrila (Lower Kachura). A 30-minute drive from town plus a 15-minute walk brings you to a pristine alpine lake surrounded by juniper forest. The water is too cold for swimming but perfect for reflection photography on windless mornings. No commercial development — bring your own snacks.

**Deosai Plateau** (day-trip from Skardu) offers the most otherworldly landscape in the region. The plateau sits at 4,114 m and is covered in wildflowers from mid-July to mid-August. The **Sheosar Lake** viewpoint is the iconic shot — a high-altitude lake framed by snow patches. Weather changes rapidly; carry warm layers even in summer.

**Sarfa Ranga Cold Desert** is a unique Skardu feature — high-altitude sand dunes at 2,200 m near the Indus. Best light is late afternoon (4:00–6:00 PM) when the dunes cast long shadows. A 4x4 is required to reach the main dune field.

**Photography tips:** K2 is visible from Skardu on roughly 30% of days — usually after a storm has cleared the air. The best K2 viewpoints are actually from the road to Askole (trekking launchpoint) or from the Shigar Valley. A 200mm+ telephoto helps compress the distance.

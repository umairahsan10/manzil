# Fairy Meadows — Local Food & Dining

Fairy Meadows is not a food destination. It is a trekking base camp with extremely limited dining options, and travellers should calibrate expectations accordingly.

**Raikot Bridge** (the jeep-track starting point) has a few basic dhabas serving daal, roti, and chai. This is your last chance for a hot, cheap meal before ascending. **Raikot Sarai** — a basic rest house near the bridge — has the most reliable food.

At **Fairy Meadows itself** (the meadow area, 3,300 m), there are roughly 15–20 wooden chalets / camping huts run by local families. Most offer a fixed meal plan: breakfast (paratha, eggs, chai), lunch (simple rice and daal or vegetable curry), dinner (chicken curry or daal with roti). The quality is basic but hearty — you are eating at 3,300 m, cooked over wood fires.

**Nanga Parbat Viewpoint Base Camp** (a 3-hour hike above the meadows) has zero food options. Bring packed lunch from your chalet.

**Practical notes:**
- Vegetarians can be accommodated if you inform your chalet host in advance
- Meat is limited and expensive to transport — chicken is more common than mutton
- No alcohol anywhere
- Bring snacks from Gilgit or Chilas — chocolate, nuts, energy bars
- Prices are high for what you get (PKR 1,500–2,500 per day for full board) because everything is carried up by porters or donkeys
- Water is from mountain streams and generally safe, but purification tablets are advisable

**The experience:** The food is not the point. Eating dal-roti by candlelight in a wooden chalet with Nanga Parbat silhouetted against the stars is the point.

# Naran — Local Food & Dining

Naran is a classic Kaghan Valley tourist town, and its food scene reflects that — heavy on desi-style BBQ, Karahi, and backpacker staples rather than hyper-local cuisine.

**The most reliable dinner spot is Lalazar Restaurant** on Main Bazaar Road. Their mutton karahi (PKR 1,200 for a full plate) is consistently good, and the tandoori chicken is a safer bet than most competitors. Expect a 20-minute wait during peak dinner hours (8:00–10:00 PM).

**Trout fishing** is Naran's culinary claim to fame. The Kunhar River is stocked with brown trout, and several restaurants along the river serve fresh-caught fish. **PTDC Naran Motel** has the most reputable trout preparation — simple pan-fried with lemon and local herbs (PKR 1,500–2,000 depending on size). Verify that the trout is actually local; some restaurants import frozen fish from Abbottabad.

**Breakfast** options are limited but functional. Most hotels include a standard Pakistani breakfast (paratha, eggs, chai). **Cafe Naran** near the bus stand serves a surprisingly decent omelette and fresh lassi.

**Street food:** The main bazaar has multiple stalls selling **corn on the cob** roasted over charcoal — a Naran tradition. Fresh apricots and plums are sold from July to September by roadside vendors. **Kulfi** (local ice cream) carts appear in the evenings.

**Practical notes:** Prices in Naran are inflated compared to southern Pakistan due to transport costs. A standard meal for two at a mid-range restaurant runs PKR 2,000–3,500. Hygiene is variable — stick to restaurants with high turnover. Tap water is glacier melt and generally safe, but bottled water is widely available.

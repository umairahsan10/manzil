# Naran — Viewpoints & Photography

Naran and the upper Kaghan Valley are defined by river, forest, and alpine lake scenery rather than towering peaks — a softer aesthetic than Hunza or Skardu.

**Saif-ul-Malook Lake** is the crown jewel. At 3,224 m, the lake sits in a glacial cirque beneath Malika Parbat (5,290 m). The classic viewpoint is from the eastern shore near the jeep drop-off point. Best light is early morning (6:00–8:00 AM) before the wind creates ripples. The lake is accessible by jeep (PKR 8,000–12,000 return from Naran) or a 6-hour trek.

**Lulusar Lake** is less visited and arguably more beautiful. It lies 45 km north of Naran on the road to Babusar Pass. The lake's turquoise water and surrounding meadow make it ideal for wide-angle landscape photography. The road is rough but passable by car in season (Jun–Sep). Go on a weekday to avoid local tour groups.

**Babusar Pass top** (4,173 m) offers the most dramatic viewpoint in the region. Looking north from the pass, you see the entire Indus Valley drainage and, on very clear days, the distant Nanga Parbat massif. The pass road is only open June–October. Morning clarity is best; afternoon clouds build by 2:00 PM.

**Naran River walk** at dawn is underrated. The Kunhar River runs milky turquoise from glacial flour, and the riverside willows catch golden side-light at sunrise. Walk upstream from the PTDC motel for the cleanest compositions.

**Photography tips:** Monsoon clouds (Jul–Aug) can create dramatic skies but also obscure peaks. A polarizing filter is essential for lake shots — it cuts reflections and reveals the turquoise depth. Mosquitoes are relentless near water at dawn and dusk; bring repellent.
